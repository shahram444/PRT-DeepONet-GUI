================================================================================
PRT-DeepONet Studio - five worked projects, start to finish
================================================================================
Follow one from the top and you end with a trained surrogate and a prediction
on a structure that was never simulated. Every box below has a value and a
reason for it.

  PROJECT 1   2D, from scratch                                    ~2 hours
  PROJECT 2   2D, from somebody else's trained network            ~1 hour
  PROJECT 3   3D, from scratch                                    ~2 hours
  PROJECT 4   3D, from data somebody else simulated               varies
  PROJECT 5   3D with the three switches                          overnight

Read the four short sections first. Everything afterwards refers back to them.


================================================================================
THE SYSTEM
================================================================================
Groundwater flows through porous rock. Dissolved acetate and sulfate arrive at
the upstream face. Sulfate-reducing bacteria in the pore space consume both and
produce sulfide, which then precipitates on the grain surfaces.

Two reactions, and they are independent settings.

  BIOTIC, in the water, catalysed by bacteria

        Ac  +  0.4 A  ->  P  +  0.04 Bio
        acetate + sulfate -> sulfide + more bacteria

    The rate is not proportional to the reactants. Bacteria saturate: past a
    point, more sulfate does not make them work faster. That is the Monod form.

        rate = k * Ac * A/(Ks_A + A) * Bio

    Ks_A is the sulfate concentration at which they run at half speed. The
    bacteria also die at 0.006 per unit time, fed or not.

  ABIOTIC, purely chemical, no microbes involved and none needed

        Ac  ->  P            rate = k_surf * Ac     (first order)

    One dissolved chemical becomes another, one mole for one. You choose which
    chemical it consumes and whether it produces the product or is a terminal
    loss. It can be made to happen only on pore voxels touching a grain, which
    is what a mineral-surface reaction does.

Each chemical is scaled by its own inlet concentration, so 1.0 means "as
concentrated as the feed". Acetate arrives at 2.0 mmol/L and sulfate at
5.0 mmol/L, so the 0.4 above is 2.0/5.0. The 0.04 is the yield.


================================================================================
CHOOSING YOUR CHEMISTRY - the three modes
================================================================================
The first decision, and it gates most of the others, is the reaction mode. It
is the box at the very top of the settings panel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BIOTIC ONLY - microbes do everything
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    The reactions in this run              biotic only
    How many chemicals                 4        Ac, A, P, Bio
    Lowest / Highest Damkohler         0.1 / 10
    Abiotic Damkohler                  not asked for, recorded as 0

Four chemicals because the biotic reaction makes a product and grows biomass,
and you want both. This is the default and the setting every published figure
in this project was made with.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ABIOTIC ONLY - a reaction with no biology in it at all
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    The reactions in this run              abiotic only
    How many chemicals                 3        Ac, A, P
    The abiotic reaction consumes      auto
    and turns it into                  product
    Lowest / Highest ABIOTIC Damkohler 0.1 / 10

THREE chemicals, not four. With no microbes there is no biomass, so a fourth
chemical would be a field of zeros taking a quarter of the network's output
head. The settings report says so before the run starts.

Leave the reactant on auto. Auto consumes the product when there is a biotic
reaction to make one and the donor when there is not, which is right in both
cases. Setting it to the product here is the trap: nothing makes the product,
so the rate multiplies a field of zeros, every run in the sweep comes out
identical, and the Damkohler number is recorded against a run it could not
affect. That combination is refused before the run starts.

What you get is Ac -> P with the two summing to the feed. Measured on a 60 by
16 channel at Peclet 10: at Da_abio 0.5 the donor leaves at 0.801 and the
product at 0.198; at 5.0 they are 0.276 and 0.723. Set "and turns it into" to
none if you want a terminal loss instead, where the reactant is consumed and
nothing tracked appears.

The acceptor A rides through as a conservative tracer. That is useful: it shows
where the water went, as distinct from where the chemistry got to.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BOTH - microbes make a product, chemistry removes it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    The reactions in this run              both
    How many chemicals                 4
    The abiotic reaction consumes      auto     (which is the product here)
    The abiotic reaction happens       only on grain surfaces
    Lowest / Highest Damkohler         0.1 / 10
    Lowest / Highest ABIOTIC Damkohler 0.1 / 10

This is the CompLaB-like case and the only one where the two reactions
interact, because the abiotic one is fed entirely by the biotic one. The two
Damkohler numbers are drawn INDEPENDENTLY, so the network can learn to tell the
two reactions apart. An earlier version wrote one number into both columns and
could not.

Set the abiotic reaction to happen only on grain surfaces if you mean
precipitation. A voxel in a narrow throat has solid on three sides and presents
three times the reactive area of one against a flat wall, and the solver
weights it accordingly.


================================================================================
THE SIX NUMBERS
================================================================================
Every simulation is described by six dimensionless numbers. A 60-micrometre
core and a 60-centimetre column with the same six behave identically, so one
trained network covers both.

  Pe        PECLET. How much faster water carries a chemical than diffusion
            spreads it: velocity x length / diffusivity. At Pe 1 solute wanders
            into every dead end; at Pe 50 it rides the fast channels and barely
            visits the slow pores. The field looks completely different, which
            is why the network must be told.

  Da_bio    BIOTIC DAMKOHLER. Reaction rate against flow: k x length /
            velocity. At 0.1 the water is through before much is consumed. At
            10 everything is eaten in the first few pores. At 1 the front sits
            mid-sample, which is the interesting case and the hardest.

  Da_abio   The same for the abiotic reaction. Independent of the biotic one.

  Ks_Ac     Monod half-saturation for acetate, over the inlet acetate. Default
            0, which means FIRST ORDER in acetate, not Monod with a zero
            constant.

  Ks_A      The same for sulfate. Default 0.15.

  Y         Biomass built per acetate consumed. Default 0.04.

WHICH DAMKOHLER. Two conventions differ by a factor of Pe. The advective one
above compares reaction against flow. The diffusive one compares it against
diffusion, Da_diff = Da_adv x Pe. The generators here use the advective form;
CompLaB's campaign builder uses the diffusive form and converts.

WHICH LENGTH. Peclet and both Damkohlers carry a length, so which length you
pick decides what all three mean. "sample" is the whole inlet-to-outlet
distance; "characteristic" is the open pore width, which is what CompLaB uses.
On a 148-voxel domain with a 16-micrometre pore they differ by 9.25 times. The
report prints the factor and converts. Both reach the solver.


================================================================================
WHAT THE NETWORK SEES
================================================================================
Three inputs.

THE PORE STRUCTURE goes into a convolutional encoder, which compresses it into
a short list of numbers that every voxel of that sample shares.

THE SIX NUMBERS go into a small network of their own, so the model can respond
to new conditions without retraining.

THE POSITION AND TIME of the point being asked about, plus the GEODESIC
DISTANCE from the inlet: not the straight-line distance, but the shortest path
a molecule could actually swim, around the grains. In a tortuous rock the two
differ by a factor of two, and the straight-line one says a molecule can reach
a spot sealed behind a grain. Everything this project claims rests on that
distinction being worth the trouble, which is why there is a switch to turn it
off and check.

The three combine to give the concentration of every chemical at that point at
that time. Ask about every pore voxel and you have the field.


================================================================================
PROJECT 1 - 2D, FROM SCRATCH
================================================================================
About two hours on your own machine. Two dimensions is a slice through the
rock, roughly fifty times cheaper than three. Settle questions about the method
here, then confirm in 3D. Set "Working in: 2D" at the top left.

STEP 0   Set up -> Check everything at once.
Ten groups, a few minutes. It tests the solvers against answers that can be
written down without them: plug flow through an open channel must leave
exp(-Da) of the inlet concentration at the outlet, and a front must spread as
wide as the exact erfc solution says. Green means the machinery works. It says
nothing about whether your science is right.

STEP 1   Set up -> Make a 2D dataset.

    Save the dataset as                work\2D_new\dataset.h5
    Where the pore structures come from   generate new ones
    How many to generate               16
    Lowest / Highest porosity          0.55 / 0.85
    Grid size (nx ny)                  148 64
    Parameter sets per structure       5
    Snapshots in time                  8
    The reactions in this run              biotic only
    How many chemicals                 4
    Lowest / Highest Peclet            3 / 50
    Lowest / Highest Damkohler         0.1 / 10
    Transport steps                    leave blank

  How many to generate. Scoring holds out whole STRUCTURES, never individual
  runs, because two runs on the same rock share its geometry. 16 gives 14 to
  learn from and 2 to be judged on: enough to catch memorising, not enough to
  quote. Use 60 for a real result.

  Porosity 0.55 to 0.85, absurdly high if you know rocks and specific to two
  dimensions: a thresholded random field stops connecting side to side at about
  0.59 in 2D and about 0.20 in 3D. In 2D a path can be blocked by going around;
  in 3D it can go over. That gap is also why a structure-reading network
  trained in 2D cannot simply be reused in 3D.

  Grid 148 x 64, which is what the published 2D work uses and what PROJECT 2
  needs.

  Snapshots spaced LOGARITHMICALLY, because filling is fast at first and then
  asymptotic: even spacing puts the whole transient in the first interval.

  Peclet 3 to 50. Below Pe 1 the problem becomes diffusion-controlled and one
  run costs about ten times more.

  Transport steps blank, so each run integrates until every chemical stops
  changing. That is the only setting right at every Peclet and Damkohler.

WHAT IS BEING SOLVED

  GEOMETRY. Smoothed white noise cut at the threshold giving your porosity.
  Pore space the inlet cannot reach becomes solid, because an isolated pocket
  has no distance-from-the-inlet.

  FLOW. Lattice-Boltzmann D2Q9, two relaxation times, body force along +x,
  halfway bounce-back on the grains. It runs until the field stops changing, so
  the iteration box is a CAP and not the number of iterations. Solved once per
  structure: Stokes flow is linear, so changing Peclet rescales the same field.

  TRANSPORT. Fixed concentration at the inlet, zero gradient at the outlet and
  the sides, no flux at the grains. At t = 0 every solute is zero and biomass
  is uniform at 0.1, because the bacteria are already there waiting to be fed.
  Biomass does not advect; it is attached. Letting it wash downstream empties
  the sample of bacteria exactly where the food is.

  NUMERICS. Second-order advection with a minmod limiter, flux-form diffusion,
  explicit in time. The limiter matters: plain upwinding adds a spurious
  diffusivity of half the local speed times the voxel size, four times the
  physical amount at Peclet 300 on a 64-voxel domain, so a run labelled Peclet
  300 behaves like Peclet 107.

  CHECKED BEFORE ANYTHING IS WRITTEN. Seven assertions on every run, covering
  the maximum principle, negative values, checkerboarding, causality, duplicate
  chemicals, blank chemicals and reaction rates with no reactant. If any run
  fails, NO FILE IS WRITTEN. A dataset that looks fine and is not is worse than
  no dataset.

STEP 2   Look at results -> Viewer -> Open Dataset.
Step through the snapshots. The front must enter on the LEFT and move right.
Then set "Show" to the geodesic distance and look at that: it is how the
network senses the rock. An empty snapshot 0 is correct.

STEP 3   Learn -> Train the network.

    Dataset                            work\2D_new\dataset.h5
    Folder for the result              work\2D_new\model
    How many passes over the data      60
    Samples per step                   8
    Voxels sampled per step            8192
    Learning rate                      0.001
    Stop after this many bad epochs    20
    What the network uses to sense geometry    gdf
    every switch                       OFF

  8192 voxels is all of them on a 148 x 64 structure, which holds about 7,000
  pore voxels. In 3D at 64 cubed there are of order 100,000 and it is a real
  sample.

  Switches OFF. This is the baseline every later claim is compared against.

Watch the Monitor tab. Only the held-out curve matters. It sits BELOW the
training curve for the first few epochs, because the training number is
averaged over the whole epoch including its clumsy beginning, and both spike
together occasionally when the optimizer steps back. Only a sustained
divergence means memorising. About 45 minutes.

STEP 4   Learn -> Evaluate a trained network. Tick "Skip the 3D renders".
Read in this order: time_model_2d.png, truth above and prediction below, and
check the front enters on the left - this picture has caught more errors than
every numerical check combined. Then rmse_vs_params.png, which shows WHERE the
model is weak rather than how weak on average, usually at high Peclet. Then
metrics.json: R-squared near 0.95 is good, RMSE around 0.05 means typical
errors of five percent of the inlet concentration.

STEP 5   Predict -> Predict on a new geometry, using a structure NOT in the
dataset, Peclet 10 and both Damkohlers 1. Under a second, against minutes for
the simulation. Check that concentrations do not exceed 1.0 and that the field
at t = 0 is zero. A network trained this way often violates both slightly,
because nothing in the loss function says concentrations are bounded. Note the
size of the violation: it is the measured argument for hard constraints.


================================================================================
PROJECT 2 - 2D, FROM SOMEBODY ELSE'S TRAINED NETWORK
================================================================================
About an hour. Needs the published 2D release in the 2D folder.

STEP 1   The published 2D release -> Browse its 3000 domains, and Its trained
models. Be clear what is there: 3,000 pore structures, several trained
networks, notebooks. There is NO simulation output. That decides which of the
next two steps applies.

STEP 2A   Their structures, our chemistry. Set up -> Make a 2D dataset with
"Where the pore structures come from" set to "use the published 2D domains" and
"How many of them to use" 200. This solves OUR reactions on their real
structures, so a 2D and a 3D result differ only in dimension. It is not their
physics, and saying so is the point. About half an hour.

STEP 2B   Their simulation output, if you can get it. Set up -> Load somebody
else's 2D simulations, with "Only report what is there" TICKED and pointed at
ONE example file. It says what it found and what is missing, and counts how
many DISTINCT structures the set holds, warning if every run used the same one,
because a split by structure is then impossible.

STEP 3   Set up -> Warm-start from the published 2D weights.

    Their trained model                2D\parameters\Monod.pt
    The grid it was trained on (nx ny) 148 64
    Chemicals in YOUR dataset          4
    Parameters in YOUR dataset         6
    Save the warm start as             work\2D_new\model\warmstart.pt

Their position-and-time network takes x, y, time and geodesic distance, exactly
what this program builds in 2D, so it transfers with no surgery. Their
parameter network takes 2 numbers where ours takes 6, so its first layer is
skipped and the tool says so rather than quietly reshaping anything.

STEP 4   Train as in PROJECT 1 but with "Start from an existing model" set to
the warm start and "freeze the reaction part" TICKED, which keeps their
reaction response and retrains only the part that reads the rock. That is the
right way round: the chemistry is the same in 2D and 3D, the pore structure is
not. Whether it helps is an open question, so run it both ways and compare the
held-out error. That comparison is a result either way.


================================================================================
PROJECT 3 - 3D, FROM SCRATCH
================================================================================
About two hours at 32 cubed, overnight at 64 cubed. Set "Working in: 3D".

STEP 1   Set up -> Make a 3D dataset.

    Save the dataset as                work\3D_new\dataset.h5
    Grid size (nx ny nz)               32 32 32
    How many pore structures           16
    Parameter sets per structure       5
    Snapshots in time                  8
    The reactions in this run              both
    How many chemicals                 4
    Lowest / Highest porosity          0.30 / 0.50
    Lowest / Highest Peclet            1 / 50
    Lowest / Highest Damkohler         0.1 / 10
    Lowest / Highest ABIOTIC Damkohler 0.1 / 10
    Random seed                        0

THE GRID IS THE EXPENSIVE CHOICE. Cost goes as roughly the fifth power: the
voxel count as the cube, and an explicit scheme needs time steps going as the
square. Measured per run on one core:

        32 cubed     7 seconds        16 structures x 5 sets:   15 minutes
        48 cubed    58 seconds                                  80 minutes
        64 cubed     4 minutes                                 5.5 hours

Prove the pipeline at 32 in an afternoon, then commit to 64 overnight. 64 is
the grid the CompLaB campaign uses, so a model trained there can be compared
against real simulation output directly.

POROSITY 0.30 to 0.50, not PROJECT 1's range: the percolation threshold is
three times lower in 3D. The number in the log reads about 0.06 higher than you
asked for, because the printed figure includes the fully-open end slices, which
on a 32-voxel domain are a fifth of the volume. The bias halves at 64.

THE PHYSICS is identical to PROJECT 1 with one dimension more: D3Q19 instead of
D2Q9, the same boundaries, the same two reactions, the same checks. That is
deliberate, so any difference between a 2D and a 3D result is about
dimensionality and not about chemistry.

ONE LIMITATION. The three Monod and yield parameters are held constant, so
three of the network's six parameter inputs carry no information and it is
blind to them. To study sensitivity to Ks, the dataset must vary it.

STEP 2   Viewer -> Open Dataset. Three orthogonal slices and a cut-away.

STEP 3   Learn -> Train the network, as in PROJECT 1 but with 8192 voxels and
every switch OFF. Check the header says

    trunk inputs: x, y, z, t, gdf

Five columns, with z present. That one line confirms you are training in three
dimensions and not on a flattened dataset. About 80 to 110 seconds an epoch at
32 cubed.

STEP 4   Learn -> Evaluate, with "Skip the 3D renders" UNTICKED. Look at
time_model_3d.png and physics_model_00_3d.png: half-cut volumes, the first
figures here that look like rock.

STEP 5   You need a structure that was never simulated. Set up -> Generate pore
geometries, 4 structures at 32 32 32, porosity 0.30 to 0.50, into
work\geometries\holdout_32, with random seed 4242.

The seed MUST differ from the dataset's. That is what guarantees these
structures are not ones the network trained on. Two defaults will hurt you:
porosity defaults to 0.20 to 0.95, far wider than any model trained here, and
the grid defaults to 128 x 64 x 64, which a 32-cubed model cannot read at all,
because the encoder's size is fixed by the grid.

Then Predict -> Predict on a new geometry with one of the new .npz files.
Leave the three voxel-count boxes blank: an .npz carries its own grid. Around
40 milliseconds.


================================================================================
PROJECT 4 - 3D FROM DATA SOMEBODY ELSE SIMULATED
================================================================================
Three situations wear the same clothes. Work out which you have first.

CASE A   Output from a CompLaB campaign. Simulate -> Collect CompLaB output
into a dataset, snapshots "transient", resampled to 11. Runs stop at different
iterations and hold different numbers of snapshots; resampling puts them on one
common normalised time axis, which is what makes the network's time input mean
the same thing in every run. Leave "skip the flow field" OFF, or the flow-proxy
and dimension-free options in PROJECT 5 cannot be used and you will collect
thousands of files twice.

Runs that crashed, produced NaN or came out all zero are skipped and REPORTED.
Read campaign_report.md; a yield well below 100% usually means something
systematic. To rehearse before a real campaign finishes, Set up -> Make a
practice CompLaB campaign writes real files in the real layout, including two
deliberately broken runs.

CASE B   You have 2D data and want to train in 3D. Simulate -> Build the 2D
transfer set extrudes a 2D structure along z. For a prismatic medium that is
EXACT, not an approximation, and the tool measures the variation along z and
prints it. Its grid must match your 3D dataset exactly. The catch is the
percolation gap from PROJECT 1: extruded data teaches chemistry well and
topology badly, so use it as a supplement of around 30%, not a replacement.

CASE C   Somebody else's trained 3D network. Put its path into "Start from an
existing model" on the Train page. The grid and the chemical names must match;
if they do not the program says which and refuses.


================================================================================
PROJECT 5 - 3D WITH THE THREE SWITCHES
================================================================================
Overnight. Do PROJECT 3 first; without its baseline none of this means
anything. Each switch is off by default, and off reproduces the original code
exactly: a test re-implements the original inside itself and compares the
arrays bit for bit.

SWITCH A   Use the flow field instead of the geometry: the ADVECTIVE TRAVEL
TIME in place of the geodesic distance, and the velocity field in place of the
pore mask. Travel time already contains what the encoder is struggling to work
out, and it is Peclet-independent because Stokes flow is linear. It may lose
below Peclet 1, where solute arrives by diffusion and a stagnant velocity field
cannot tell a deep dead-end pore from solid rock: travel time is infinite in
both, while the geodesic distance still distinguishes them. Setting it to
"both" keeps the geodesic distance as well and is the safe option.

SWITCH B   Mix in 2D data, typically 30%. See PROJECT 4 CASE B.

SWITCH C   Dimension-free coordinates: travel time, distance to the nearest
wall, and time. Three inputs in 2D and three in 3D, so the same network fits
both and a model trained on cheap 2D data can be applied to 3D. It turns on
SWITCH A automatically because it needs the travel time. The price is that the
network no longer knows where it is in the sample, only how far along the flow
and how far from a wall.

RUNNING THE COMPARISON. Learn -> Run the switch comparison, with 300 epochs per
configuration. Tick "Quick plumbing check only" ONCE first: three epochs, tiny
batches, proving it runs and nothing else. Then untick and leave it overnight.

It trains the baseline, the Euclidean control, flow-with-travel-time,
flow-with-both, dimension-free and the two 2D-transfer variants, scores every
one on the SAME held-out structures, and splits the error BY PECLET BAND. That
breakdown is the point. Write down your prediction before you look: the flow
field should win at high Peclet and lose below 1. If your dataset has no runs
below Peclet 1, the comparison cannot test half of that, which is the argument
for building one with the lowest Peclet at 0.3.


================================================================================
WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG
================================================================================
The settings panel prints four kinds of remark and they mean different things.

  NOTES                     something overrode a default. Informational.
  LEGAL, BUT PROBABLY NOT   the run will start. Read these: a blank chemical,
  WHAT YOU MEANT            two chemicals that will be identical, a biomass in
                            a run with no microbes.
  PROBLEMS                  nothing is simulated until you fix it.
  PHYSICS run N             a finished run failed a check on its own output.
                            No file is written.

The three faults you are most likely to hit, in the order they appear.

  A run stops at the step cap. Raise the transport step cap to the number the
  banner prints. Low-Peclet runs need it most.

  A chemical is a rescaled copy of another. Two chemicals have nothing to tell
  them apart. Give one a different diffusion coefficient, or reduce the count.

  A Damkohler number whose reactant is zero everywhere. The reaction had
  nothing to act on. Check the reaction mode and which chemical the abiotic
  reaction consumes.

The Problems tab collects every error with the command that produced it, and
the same command appears on every action page before you press Run, so you can
paste it into a terminal. The usual causes are a path that does not exist yet,
a dataset built at a different grid size than the model expects, a switch on
with no velocity field at prediction time, and a checkpoint and dataset that
disagree about the chemicals.

Set up -> Check everything at once is the fastest way to tell a broken
installation from a broken idea.
